- **Learning:**
  - The most valuable finding was structural, not textual. The original file ran through unnumbered sections and then jumped straight to `13. Orchestration and Execution Protocol` and `14. Human Approval Gates`. Those surviving numbers were the evidence that sections 1 through 12 had been planned and lost, and that the sections the issue's acceptance criteria demanded (coding conventions, testing, documentation) were among the missing ones. A typo hunt alone would have missed this entirely; the numbering gap identified the actual scope of the gap.
  - The passages carried over from an unrelated project were not cosmetic. Left in place they would have instructed agents to check for privacy controls around a domain that does not exist in QuantRule, and to gate changes to a model that does not exist either. Removing them required rewriting the affected commit-message examples and one approval-gate example rather than deleting single words, because each was embedded in a rule that still needed to survive.
  - The human directed that the removed text be described only as eliminated, with no restatement of what it was, so that neither the repository, the history, nor the logs carry it forward. This plan and this log follow that instruction: the fact of the removal is the record, not the content. The practical consequence is that a future reader cannot audit the removal by comparing against the original text, and the executable check — a repository-wide and history-wide scan returning no matches — is what replaces that audit trail.
  - The issue asked for "technology-agnostic" instructions while the document also has to name Rust. These were reconciled by reading "technology-agnostic" as *no mandated libraries or frameworks*, not *no language*, and by keeping the language declaration plus the rule to verify installed toolchain versions before relying on version-specific behavior. This interpretation is recorded in the execution plan as an assumption so a reviewer can challenge it. The alternative reading, dropping the language entirely, was rejected because it would make the guidelines less actionable and contradict the project's own stated implementation language.
  - The surviving `13.` and `14.` headings could have been renumbered down to `11.` and `12.` to make the document shorter. They were not, because leaving the original numbers makes the diff against the previous file easier to follow and signals that the intended structure was always fourteen sections.
  - The document grew to roughly 530 lines against an acceptance criterion of "concise". This was accepted deliberately: the agent role definitions are approximately 180 of those lines, and the approval gates add about 30 more, and they encode the governance model the project relies on. Compressing them would shorten the file at the cost of the model. The tension and the reversal path (splitting the role definitions into a separate document) are recorded in the execution plan rather than left implicit.
